Understanding Confabulation and Rethinking Reconstruction in Activation Explanations

Gert Lek1, Zixuan Xia2, Pin-Yu Chen3, Lydia Chen1

1 Université de Neuchâtel · 2 Universität Bern · 3 International Business Machines (IBM)

Paper↗Code↗NLA Evaluations↗Original NLA↗
The short version

Point-reconstruction training makes NLA explanations more useful, but also more confabulated and less well written. Flow-NLA replaces point reconstruction with distributional denoising feedback, retaining substantial utility gains while curbing the growth of confabulation and writing defects.

01 Motivation

From an activation
to an explanation.

A Natural Language Autoencoder (NLA) learns to describe a model’s internal activation in ordinary language. A verbalizer writes an explanation; a reconstructor tries to recover the activation from that text.

Reconstruction quality supplies the verbalizer’s learning signal.

After supervised initialization, better reconstruction provides an unsupervised reward for the explanation. The target language model stays frozen. But what does that reward encourage the explanation to say?

The empirical problem

Better reconstruction does not
necessarily mean better explanations.

In the point-reconstruction runs studied here, reconstruction improves alongside recoverable, instance-specific information. Yet contextual confabulation and writing defects also increase. This is an observed training pattern; whether the coupling is necessary remains open.

Useful information still increases

More specific.
Not necessarily more supported.

Hard-negative separation compares an explanation with one from a closely related source that differs in the answer-relevant fact. Both training objectives increasingly separate the two.

A larger separation can reflect useful matched explanations, misleading mismatches, or both. It is not an accuracy score.

02 Evaluation

Three questions.
Three views of explanation quality.

Information, source support, and writing are evaluated separately. Each judge receives only the context its task requires.

A

Utility

What can a reader recover?

Sees: explanation + task query

  • Classification: accuracy across 12 source-property tasks.
  • Suffix selection: select the true 32-token continuation from ten candidates.
  • Response behavior: answer, refusal, or clarification; macro-F1.
  • Hard-negative separation: correct-answer log-probability ratio for matched versus mismatched explanations.
The source and activation are withheld.
B

Source support

Are the claims supported?

Sees: source + explanation

Atomic claims are labeled supported, contradicted, or unsupported.

  • Assertion Risk: proportion of assessed assertions that are unsupported or contradicted.
  • Confabulated Token Coverage: proportion of explanation tokens covered by those claims.
Future-text predictions and hidden-computation claims are not assessed and are excluded from both metrics.
C

Writing quality

Is the explanation well written?

Sees: explanation only

  • Clarity: unclear wording, malformed or unfinished sentences.
  • Coherence: statements that conflict within the explanation.
  • Economy: avoidable repetition or filler.
Distinct defects per explanation, with exact evidence spans.

All three dimensions use a separate judge LLM, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash. Source support measures contextual evidence; it does not establish mechanistic faithfulness. Adapted from the paper’s evaluation overview.

03 The information blind spot

Point reconstruction only sees
the conditional mean.

An explanation can narrow the set of possible activations without moving their mean. Under NLA’s normalized squared-error objective, that information cannot improve the optimal point-reconstruction reward.

Optimal point reconstruction · unit-norm activations and predictions

min E[‖H − u‖² | Z = z] = 2 − 2‖μᵩ(z)‖, with ‖u‖ = 1

Here μᵩ(z) = Eᵩ[H | Z = z]. Only the conditional mean determines this optimal expected loss.

  1. 01

    Same mean

    The explanation rules out vertical directions. The remaining pair still has mean zero.

  2. 02

    Partial observation

    A noisy activation leaves the true and upward candidates similarly plausible.

  3. 03

    Useful denoising

    The explanation removes the upward candidate. The estimate moves toward the true activation.

04 The method

Flow-NLA.
Reconstruction beyond the mean.

Instead of predicting one activation from an explanation, Flow-NLA models the distribution of activations compatible with it. The explanation earns reward when it helps denoise the observed activation across noise levels.

01 / Explain

Write from the activation.

The verbalizer maps the activation h to an explanation z.

02 / Denoise

Recover from partial evidence.

Gaussian noise corrupts the standardized activation x. The reconstructor predicts a velocity from (xt, t, z), which determines a noise prediction.

03 / Reward

Credit informative explanations.

Integrated denoising error defines a likelihood-bound-derived reward used in the verbalizer’s group-relative update.

The denoising objective, in two equations +

After fixed coordinate standardization, add Gaussian noise along a variance-preserving path:

xₜ = aₜx + bₜε, vₜ = aₜε − bₜx, ε ∼ N(0, I), aₜ² + bₜ² = 1.

The flow reconstructor predicts the velocity. Its implied noise prediction is ε̂θ = bₜxₜ + aₜfθ(xₜ, t, z). The verbalizer’s reward is:

Rθ(h,z) = −½ ∫₋₈⁸ Eε ‖ε − ε̂θ(xλ, λ, z)‖² dλ.

Here λ = log(aₜ² / bₜ²) indexes noise levels. The reward is the explanation-dependent term of a diffusion likelihood bound, log pθ(h|z) ≥ Rθ(h,z) − c(h), where c(h) is independent of the explanation. Numerical integration and sampled noise approximate this term.

05 Results

Retain utility.
Limit the growth of confabulation.

Across Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, Gemma-3-12B-it, and Apertus-v1.5-8B, Flow-NLA follows similar utility trajectories to point-reconstruction NLA, with substantially less growth in confabulation and writing defects.

What these results establish

Richer reconstruction feedback is a promising direction, not a guarantee of source support or mechanistic faithfulness. The study covers the reported models, tasks, and training procedures, relies on automated judgments, and includes provider and inference differences that limit causal attribution to the objective alone. The distribution-identification analysis assumes optimal denoisers and a fixed verbalizer.
